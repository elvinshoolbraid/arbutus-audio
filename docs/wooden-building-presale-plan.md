# Community-built timber apartment building: presale, open design, legal

Status: planning spec and grilling. No implementation. No site, no entity, no money taken.
Jurisdiction assumed: British Columbia, Canada (Victoria). Everything legal here is a map for a
lawyer conversation, not advice. Dated 2026-09-25.

## 1. What is being asked for, in one paragraph

Run a presale for a mass-timber apartment building the way Arbutus-1 is being run: one honest public
log, refundable reservation deposits the way Tesla took Model 3 reservations, a self-hosted
Kickstarter-style campaign instead of Kickstarter, and a design that is developed in the open so
people can contribute and improve it. Use German timber housing as the scale precedent and open
source projects as the precedent for how contribution, licensing and messaging work.

## 2. The first finding: this is three projects wearing one coat

Every hard question below comes from the same root. "A presale for an open-source community-built
building" bundles three things that live under three different legal regimes and need three different
owners. Separate them on paper before doing anything else.

| Layer | What it is | Who owns it | Legal regime |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. The design commons | A reusable mass-timber apartment building system: drawings, models, specs, cost data, lessons | A non-profit society or just a licensed public repository | Copyright and open licences, trademark, professional practice acts |
| B. The building | One specific building on one specific lot in one municipality | A development entity: housing co-op, or a corporation or limited partnership | Zoning, building code, REDMA (presales), Homeowner Protection Act, construction lending |
| C. The money and the crowd | Reservations, deposits, updates, Instagram, the mailing list | The development entity, with deposits held by a lawyer in trust | REDMA, securities law, consumer protection, privacy |

The commons can start today with zero legal risk beyond a licence choice. The building cannot start
until there is land. The money cannot start until there is an entity and a lawyer, and in BC it
cannot take deposits on units until a disclosure statement is filed. The plan is therefore staged so
that each layer only takes on obligations it can honour.

## 3. Precedents, and what to take from each

### 3.1 Scale precedents (Germany and BC)

The reference in the brief was "the world's largest wooden apartment building" by a German builder.
The name did not resolve cleanly, so here is what actually exists. If the intended precedent was a
different project, replace this table.

| Project | Where | What it proves | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roots | Hamburg | Germany's tallest timber residential building, about 65 m, 18 floors, completed 2024 | [ubm magazin](https://www.ubm-development.com/magazin/en/back-to-the-roots/) |
| Skaio | Heilbronn | Germany's first timber high-rise apartment building (2019) | [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/949490/skaio-wooden-apartment-building-kaden-plus-lager) |
| Lynarstrasse | Berlin | One of Germany's largest solid-wood residential buildings, 98 units, built by a co-op | [binderholz](https://www.binderholz.com/en-us/mass-timber-solutions/residential-building-lynarstrasse-berlin-germany/) |
| Prinz-Eugen-Park | Munich | Largest contiguous timber housing settlement in Europe, about 600 units, many by Baugruppen and co-ops | [binderholz](https://www.binderholz.com/en-us/mass-timber-solutions/quartier-prinz-eugen-park-munich-germany/) |
| WoHo | Berlin | Planned tallest timber building in Germany; shows the ambition ceiling and the delays | [ubm magazin](https://www.ubm-development.com/magazin/en/woho-berlin/) |
| Brock Commons Tallwood House | UBC, Vancouver | 18-storey mass timber student housing (2017), the local proof that this height works in BC | (widely documented; verify current status) |

The BC Building Code 2024 allows encapsulated mass timber up to 18 storeys for residential, with more
exposed timber permitted in buildings up to eight storeys. Source:
[BC Gov News](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2024HOUS0055-000522),
[AIBC](https://aibc.ca/2024/05/bc-codes-update-for-mass-timber-construction/).

Take from these: the German large projects were mostly not built by lone founders. They were built by
co-ops and Baugruppen (self-organised building groups) with professional developers and a municipal
land policy that favoured them. Munich's timber quarter exists because the city sold land to groups
with conditions. That is the real precedent: the community model needs a land pathway, not just
enthusiasm.

### 3.2 The "licensed system" precedent: Cree by Rhomberg

Cree Buildings (Austria, founded 2010) developed a timber-hybrid system for buildings up to about
100 m and licenses it to partners who share knowledge through a platform. Source:
[Cree Buildings](https://www.creebuildings.com/system/),
[Rhomberg](https://www.rhomberg.com/en/services/construction/modular-construction/hybrid-timber-construction-cree-buildings).
This is the closest thing to a reusable "building system with a community of builders", but it is
proprietary and licensed, not open. It is worth studying for what a system needs to contain
(standard connections, floor cassettes, fire and acoustic assemblies, cost models) to be reusable
across sites.

### 3.3 The open design precedent: WikiHouse

WikiHouse publishes its building system under CC0. Contributors own what they make and choose to
share it; WikiHouse owes them nothing and they owe WikiHouse nothing. The terms disclaim liability
for any harm from using the designs, except where law forbids that (death or personal injury from
negligence). The trademark is licensed separately to chapters. Source:
[WikiHouse terms](https://www.wikihouse.cc/terms).

Take from this: the pattern is "designs are free, the name is controlled, nobody is liable for your
build, and a licensed professional must still sign your actual building". That last clause is the
whole trick and it is what makes open building design compatible with the law.

### 3.4 The community finance precedent: Mietshäuser Syndikat

The Syndikat (Germany, since 1999, over 160 projects) finances houses with low-interest direct loans
from supporters, repaid from rents; each house is a company co-owned by the residents' association
and the Syndikat, which holds a veto on resale. Source:
[syndikat.org direct loans](https://www.syndikat.org/en/direct-loans/),
[Frontiers paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-cities/articles/10.3389/frsc.2022.759332/full).

Take from this: the veto-against-resale structure and the "money from people who know the project"
idea. Do not take the direct-loan mechanism literally. In Canada a loan from the public is a security
and needs an exemption (section 6.2 below).

### 3.5 The reservation precedent: Tesla Model 3

Tesla took US$1,000 refundable reservations in 2016 for a car with no announced delivery date, took
several hundred thousand in a week, and then delivered late by years for most people. It worked
because Tesla already had shipped two cars and a public track record, the deposit was small relative
to the price, refunds were honoured, and updates were frequent. It also became a lesson in how
"honest timelines" erode when the audience is large. The equivalent "already shipped product" for
this project does not exist yet. That gap is the first grilling question.

### 3.6 Why not Kickstarter itself

Kickstarter rewards cannot be equity or debt, pledges are non-refundable and all-or-nothing, and the
creator must fulfil or refund. Source: [Kickstarter rules](https://www.kickstarter.com/rules).
A refundable deposit toward a home is the opposite of that model in every respect. Self-hosting is
correct. The lesson to keep from Kickstarter is the funding goal with a deadline: publish a number,
a date, and what happens if the number is not met.

## 4. Architecture

### 4.1 Entity and money map

```
   Public / community
        |  reads, comments, contributes designs
        v
+---------------------------+       licence + DCO        +-----------------------------+
| A. Design commons         | <------------------------- | Contributors                |
| public repo, open licence |                            | (anyone, no obligation)     |
| owned by a society or     |                            +-----------------------------+
| by Elvin under licence    |
+------------+--------------+
             | designs are input, never authority
             v
+---------------------------+   stamped drawings    +-----------------------------+
| B. Development entity     | <-------------------- | Architect of record (AIBC)  |
| co-op or corp/LP          |                       | Engineer of record (EGBC)   |
| owns land, holds permits, |                       | Licensed builder (HPO)      |
| is the "developer" under  |                       +-----------------------------+
| REDMA                     |
+------------+--------------+
             | reservation and deposit agreements
             v
+---------------------------+
| C. Trust account          |   refundable on demand until conversion
| lawyer or notary          |   converts to REDMA deposit or co-op share
+---------------------------+   only at a defined gate
```

The rule that holds the whole thing together: money never touches the commons, and the commons never
touches the building's legal drawings except through a licensed professional who adopts them.

### 4.2 Stage gates for the presale

Each stage has an entry condition, what a supporter gets, and what money is allowed. Nothing in a
later stage may be promised in an earlier one.

| Stage | Entry condition | What supporters can do | Money allowed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Interest list | Site live, log started | Sign up, follow, contribute to the commons | None. Not even "hold my spot" money. |
| 1. Founding supporter | Entity incorporated, lawyer retained, trust account open | Pay a small refundable reservation for a numbered place in the queue | Small fixed amount, fully refundable on request, held in trust, not tied to any unit or price |
| 2. Site announced | Land under option or contract, zoning path known | Convert queue place to a named site; see the pro forma | Same deposit; still refundable |
| 3. Disclosure statement filed | REDMA disclosure statement filed with BCFSA; early marketing clock starts | Sign a purchase agreement (condo model) or membership subscription (co-op model) | Deposits up to REDMA limits, in trust; 7-day rescission; full refund if building permit and financing are not reached inside the early-marketing window |
| 4. Building permit and financing | Permit issued, construction loan committed | Deposits become firm per the agreement | Per the agreement |
| 5. Construction | Shovels in | Site visits, monthly log, cost reporting | Progress draws from lender, not from depositors |
| 6. Occupancy | Occupancy permit, warranty in place | Move in | Balance on closing |

Stage 1 is where the brief's "refundable deposit like Tesla" lives, and it is the stage a lawyer must
bless before it opens (see section 6.1). The honest form of it is: "you are paying to be early in a
queue for a project that may not happen; you can have your money back any time by asking".

### 4.3 The honest-timeline mechanism

Honesty is a process, not a promise. Specify it as one:

- The plan is published as a dependency graph (section 5), not a Gantt chart. Each node has an owner,
  a status, and a date range with a stated confidence. Slips are edits to the graph, with a log entry
  saying what slipped and why.
- A fixed update cadence, published and kept: one log post a month minimum, the same day each month,
  even if the post is "nothing moved, here is why". The Arbutus-1 log is the template: dated, newest
  first, photos, plain language, no marketing voice.
- Every number that supporters rely on (deposit count, money in trust, target unit price, cost per
  square metre, schedule) is published in one table on the site and updated on the same cadence. If a
  number is unknown, the table says "unknown" rather than an estimate.
- Instagram carries the same content as the log, never more. A post that promises something the log
  does not is a bug.
- A public "what would kill this" list, updated monthly, ranked. The first version is section 7.

### 4.4 The contribution architecture (the commons)

- One public repository per building system, not per building. Contents: architectural and structural
  models in an open format (IFC), 2D drawings as PDF and DXF, assembly details, fire and acoustic
  assemblies with their test references, a cost model spreadsheet, a decision log, and lessons from any
  built project.
- Contributions arrive as proposals against a written RFC (request for comments) process: problem,
  proposed change, evidence, who reviewed it. Merging is a design decision by a named maintainer, not
  a vote.
- A Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO, a one-line "I have the right to contribute this" sign-off)
  rather than a contributor licence agreement. It is lighter, and it is what WikiHouse's "you owe us
  nothing" model implies.
- The "engineer of record" boundary is written into the README: nothing in the repo is a permit
  drawing; nothing in it is sealed; using it for a real building requires a licensed professional in
  your jurisdiction to adopt and seal it. The project-specific sealed set lives in the development
  entity's files, not in the commons, and is published to the commons only after occupancy and only
  with the sealing professional's consent.
- Two branches of work: `system` (reusable, licensed, open) and `project-<site>` (specific, includes
  things that can only be published after the fact). Community contribution targets `system`.
- What contributors get: named credit in the repo and the built building, early queue access to
  later projects, and nothing else that could be read as compensation or equity. Say this in the
  first sentence of the contributing guide.

### 4.5 Platform

No custom software until a stage gate needs it.

- Stage 0: a static site in the arbutus.audio pattern (HTML, CSS, no framework), a mailing list, the
  public repo, Instagram.
- Stage 1: add the reservation form and a payment processor that supports easy refunds, with the
  money landing in the lawyer's trust account, not a company account. A spreadsheet is the queue.
- Stage 3 onward: the developer's lawyer runs the agreements. The site only reports.

## 5. Dependencies

The critical path is land, then zoning, then money, then permit. Community and design work run in
parallel and do not shorten it.

| Dependency | Blocks | Owner | Typical lead time | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A developer of record with a track record a lender will accept | Construction financing, REDMA credibility | Elvin plus a partner or a mentor developer | Months to find | Missing |
| Land: a lot, an option, or a municipal or non-profit land partner | Everything in layer B | Development entity | 6 to 24 months | Missing |
| Zoning that permits the height and use, or a rezoning path | Development permit | Municipality | 12 to 36 months if rezoning | Unknown until site |
| Architect of record (AIBC registered) | Development permit, building permit | Development entity | Retain early | Missing |
| Structural engineer with mass timber experience (EGBC registered) | Building permit, cost certainty | Development entity | Retain early | Missing |
| Code consultant for encapsulated mass timber | Building permit | Development entity | With design | Missing |
| Licensed residential builder and 2-5-10 warranty (Homeowner Protection Act) | Sale of any new home | Development entity | Before construction | Missing |
| Timber supplier and fabricator (CLT or glulam) | Cost model, schedule | Development entity | Long lead on capacity | Missing |
| Lawyer for REDMA and securities | Any money | Development entity | Weeks to retain | Missing |
| Entity choice: housing co-op vs strata corporation vs rental | Everything in layer C | Elvin | A decision, not a lead time | Undecided |
| Construction lender, or CMHC or BC Housing program if co-op or rental | Stage 4 | Development entity | 6 to 12 months | Missing |
| Open licence and trademark for the commons | Layer A launch | Elvin | Days | Undecided |
| Public site, log, mailing list | Stage 0 | Elvin | Days | Can start now |

Two dependencies dominate. Land is the one everyone underestimates: without a site there is nothing to
reserve, and BC law prohibits taking deposits on units that do not have a filed disclosure statement,
which itself needs a site. The developer-of-record problem is the one nobody says out loud: a lender
finances a person or firm with completed projects, not a plan. The German precedents solved this with
professional co-op developers and city land. The BC analogue is a partnership with a non-profit
housing provider, a community land trust, or an established developer who wants a timber showcase.

## 6. Legal map (for the lawyer meeting)

### 6.1 Presales: Real Estate Development Marketing Act (REDMA), BC

- A developer must file a disclosure statement with BCFSA before marketing a development unit, and
  before using any agreement or taking any deposit. Advertising a proposed development is allowed
  before filing, but only if nobody gets the impression they have a right to acquire a unit, and each
  ad should carry a disclaimer that it is not an offering. Source:
  [BCFSA presales](https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/information/presales-information),
  [Boughton Law FAQ](https://www.boughtonlaw.com/2024/03/frequently-asked-questions-real-estate-development-marketing-regulations/).
- Early marketing under Policy Statements 5 and 6: after filing, the developer has 12 months to get
  a building permit and a satisfactory financing commitment. If not, purchasers can cancel and get a
  full refund. Deposits are capped at 10% until the amendment is delivered. A pilot extends the window
  to 18 months for 100-plus-unit projects. Sources:
  [Bell Alliance](https://www.bellalliance.ca/2023/10/30/early-marketing-for-development-projects/),
  [BCFSA advisory](https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-professional-resources/knowledge-base/advisory/new-consumer-disclosure-requirement-pre-sales-and-launch-early-marketing-extension-pilot-program).
- Deposits are held in trust by a lawyer, notary or brokerage. Purchasers have a 7-day rescission
  right after receiving the disclosure statement.
- Consequence for the plan: the Tesla-style Stage 1 deposit cannot be a deposit "on a unit". It has
  to be legally something else: a refundable reservation for queue position in a future offering,
  with explicit language that no unit, price, or right to buy is being offered. Whether even that is
  permissible before a disclosure statement is filed is the first question for the lawyer. If the
  answer is no, Stage 1 collapses into Stage 0 (a list with no money) and the "motivation from money
  deposited" has to come from somewhere else, such as a co-op share (below).

### 6.2 Securities: raising money from the crowd

- Any loan, share, or profit-sharing interest sold to the public is a security. Canada has a
  start-up crowdfunding exemption (NI 45-110) with a cap of $1.5 million per issuer per 12 months,
  and low per-investor caps; it requires a funding portal. Source:
  [BCSC news release](https://www.bcsc.bc.ca/about/media-room/news-releases/2021/45-canadian-securities-regulators-adopt-new-nationally-harmonized-start-up-crowdfunding-rules),
  [NI 45-110 text](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/243_2021).
  $1.5 million does not build an apartment building. It might fund land option, design and permits.
- A BC cooperative association has its own exemption (BC Instrument 45-530) for issuing membership
  and investment shares to members and prospective members, subject to conditions. Source:
  [BCSC guide to capital raising for co-ops](https://www.bcsc.bc.ca/-/media/PWS/New-Resources/Securities-Law/Instruments-and-Policies/BCN/Guide-for-Capital-Raising-for-Cooperative-Associations-June-2-2020.pdf).
  This is the most natural legal home for "people put money in early and are motivated by it": a
  membership share in a housing co-op is refundable per the co-op's rules, is not a REDMA unit sale,
  and matches the Lynarstrasse and Syndikat precedents in spirit. It also changes the product: members
  get a right to occupy, not a title to a condo.
- Direct loans from supporters (the Syndikat model) would need an exemption, most likely the offering
  memorandum exemption or the co-op instrument. Do not copy the German mechanism without this.

### 6.3 Entity choice determines the whole legal shape

| Model | Supporter gets | Presale regime | Financing | Fit with "community built" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strata condo | Title to a unit | REDMA, full | Presales unlock the construction loan; needs a developer with a record | Weak: buyers, not members |
| Housing co-op | Membership share and right to occupy | Cooperative Association Act plus BCI 45-530; REDMA may still apply to some structures, ask | CMHC and BC Housing programs exist for co-ops; slower but designed for groups | Strong: the German precedents are this |
| Purpose-built rental | A lease later | No presale; the crowd funds only the commons | Rental lenders, CMHC MLI Select | Medium: supporters fund design, not homes |

The brief's language ("presale", "deposit", "motivation from money deposited") points at condo. The
brief's values ("community built", "collaborative creation", German precedents) point at co-op. This
is the decision that most needs to be made before the lawyer meeting, and it is Elvin's alone.

### 6.4 Professional practice and building law

- Under BC's Architects Act and the Professional Governance Act, permit drawings for a building of
  this size must be sealed by a registered architect and engineers. A mechanical engineering student
  cannot seal anything and must not describe the commons as "plans" or "designs for a building" in a
  way that implies professional practice. The commons is "reference design documentation".
- The Homeowner Protection Act requires a licensed residential builder and 2-5-10 home warranty
  insurance for new homes offered for sale. A community cannot build its own homes for sale without
  this.
- Municipal: development permit, building permit, and in Victoria a design review process. Mass
  timber at height needs a code consultant and usually an alternative solutions report.

### 6.5 The commons: licence, trademark, contribution, liability

- Licence: CERN-OHL-S 2.0 (strongly reciprocal) if the goal is that improvements to the system come
  back to the commons; CC BY-SA 4.0 is the more familiar choice for drawings and documents and works
  the same way in practice; CC0 (WikiHouse) if the goal is maximum spread and no obligations. Sources:
  [CERN OHL](https://ospo.docs.cern.ch/recommendations/licensing/),
  [WikiHouse terms](https://www.wikihouse.cc/terms).
  Recommendation: CC BY-SA for documentation and drawings, CERN-OHL-S for anything that is a
  manufacturable component design. Decide before the first public commit; relicensing later needs
  every contributor's consent.
- Contribution: DCO sign-off on every contribution. No CLA. A contributing guide that says, in order:
  you keep your copyright, you licence it under the project licence, you get credit, you get nothing
  else, and nothing here is a sealed drawing.
- Liability: copy the shape of the WikiHouse disclaimer (no liability for use of the designs, except
  where law forbids the exclusion) and have the lawyer localise it. Add that the commons is not a
  professional service.
- Trademark: register the project name. The designs are free; the name is not. This is how every open
  hardware project stops a bad build from carrying its name.
- Messaging precedent to copy: WikiHouse's "you owe us nothing, we owe you nothing" and the Syndikat's
  "the house belongs to those who live in it". Both are one-sentence promises that survive contact
  with lawyers.

### 6.6 Consumer, privacy, advertising

- Refund terms for Stage 1 must be written, published, and honoured on request without conditions.
  BC's Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act applies to prepayments and to representations.
- The mailing list and deposit records fall under BC's Personal Information Protection Act: say what
  is collected and why, and do not put the list in a US tool without checking.
- Every Instagram post about the building is "marketing" under REDMA once a specific development is
  identifiable. Before the disclosure statement is filed, every such post needs the "this is not an
  offering" disclaimer or must be about the commons only.

## 7. Spec: what the presale program is

Requirements language. MUST is a hard rule; SHOULD is the default unless a reason is logged.

1. The programme MUST publish its stage gates (section 4.2) and MUST NOT accept any money before the
   lawyer has confirmed the Stage 1 instrument in writing.
2. Every Stage 1 reservation MUST be refundable on request, in full, within 10 business days, with no
   reason required, and held in a lawyer's trust account.
3. The reservation MUST NOT reference a unit, a price, a floor, a size, or a date. It MAY reference a
   queue position.
4. The site MUST show, on one page: money in trust, number of reservations, refunds issued, and the
   current dependency graph with dates and confidence. Updated at least monthly on a fixed day.
5. Every schedule slip MUST get a log entry naming the cause, and the graph MUST be edited the same
   day.
6. The programme MUST publish a kill list (top risks) and a kill criterion: the condition under which
   all reservations are refunded and the project stops.
7. Instagram MUST carry only content already in the log or the commons, and MUST carry the REDMA
   disclaimer on any post that identifies a development.
8. The commons MUST be licensed and carry the professional-practice disclaimer before its first
   public commit. Contributions MUST carry a DCO sign-off.
9. Sealed project drawings MUST NOT be committed to the commons until after occupancy and the
   sealing professional's written consent.
10. Contributors MUST NOT receive anything that could be construed as equity, debt, discount, or
    priority right of purchase. Credit and queue access for future projects only.
11. The entity model (co-op, strata, rental) MUST be decided and stated publicly before Stage 1 opens.
12. The programme SHOULD name a second person with authority over the trust account instructions so
    refunds do not depend on one person being available.
13. The programme SHOULD have a partner developer or non-profit housing provider named before Stage 2.

## 8. Grilling

These are the questions a serious investor, a lender, a lawyer, or a sceptical supporter will ask.
A good answer is written in the log; a bad answer is "we'll figure it out". Ordered by how much
depends on the answer.

### Existence

1. What is the Model 3 here? Tesla had two shipped cars and a Supercharger network when it took
   reservations. What has this project shipped that makes a stranger hand over money?
2. Who is the developer of record? Which person or firm, with what completed buildings, will a
   lender name on the construction loan? If the answer is "me", what is the plan to change that?
3. Where is the land? Not "we will find a site": which municipality, which zoning, which owner, and
   why would they sell or partner? Has anyone called them?
4. What is the number that makes the building go: how many reservations, converting at what rate,
   into what deposit, unlocks what loan? If that number is unknown, what is being sold?
5. Are these homes people own, memberships people hold, or rentals people wait for? Pick one and
   accept its legal regime. Every dodge here is a future lawsuit.

### Money and trust

6. What, legally, is the Stage 1 deposit? A reservation for queue position, a co-op share, a
   donation, or a REDMA deposit? Has a BC lawyer said the chosen form is lawful before a disclosure
   statement is filed?
7. Why would anyone deposit before a site is known? What do they get that a free mailing list does
   not give them? If the honest answer is "they want to signal support", is a deposit the right
   instrument or is a co-op membership drive?
8. Who can move money out of the trust account, and what happens to refunds if that person is
   unreachable for a month?
9. What is the kill criterion? At what date or condition does the project refund everyone and stop,
   and is it published before the first dollar arrives?
10. If costs come in 40% over the cost model (normal for first timber buildings), who eats it: the
    depositors, the entity, or the project dies? Say which, in writing, in advance.
11. What do reservations cost the project? Every refundable dollar is a liability that must be
    reported and cannot be spent. Is the plan clear that this money funds nothing?

### Honesty mechanics

12. What is the first date that will be published, and what confidence is attached to it? If the
    answer is a single date, the timeline is not honest yet.
13. Who writes the monthly post if Elvin is in exams, or sick, or has moved on? Honesty that depends
    on one person's mood is a promise, not a mechanism.
14. When Instagram growth pressures the message toward hype, what is the rule that stops it? Name the
    rule and the person who enforces it.
15. What is the one-sentence promise? If it takes a paragraph, it will not survive.

### The commons

16. Why is the design open? A building is built once; the value of open design is the second
    building. Who builds the second one, and what do they need from the commons to do it?
17. What does a contributor actually contribute before there is a site? Without a site there is no
    programme, no code path, no loads. The reusable parts (connections, floor cassettes, assemblies,
    cost model) are engineering. Who reviews them, with what authority?
18. What do contributors get, and is the answer "nothing that looks like money" written down before
    the first contribution arrives?
19. Which licence, decided before the first public commit? Relicensing later needs every
    contributor's consent.
20. How is the line drawn between the open reference design and the sealed drawings, and what stops a
    supporter from believing they are the same thing?
21. What stops someone from building a bad version and calling it by this name? Is the trademark
    registered?

### The person

22. Is this a second project alongside Arbutus-1, or a replacement for it? Two public build logs from
    one person is a credibility problem for both.
23. What does the mechanical engineering degree contribute here, and where does the missing
    competence (development, architecture, structural timber, construction law, finance) come from?
    Name the mentor or partner for each.
24. What is the smallest version of this that is real? A four-storey, twelve-unit co-op on a
    non-profit's land is a building. An eighteen-storey showcase is a fundraising story. Which one is
    being started?
25. What would make you stop, and would you say so publicly?

## 9. Decisions this document needs before anything else moves

Not tasks. Decisions.

- Entity model: co-op, strata, or rental. Recommendation from this analysis: co-op, because it matches
  every precedent cited, it has its own securities exemption for member money, and it makes
  "community built" true rather than a slogan. Cost: the product is a right to occupy, not a title.
